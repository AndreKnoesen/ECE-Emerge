Fact Sheet

  • A linear system has two important properties:

    • Scaling: if input \(x\) produces output \(y\), then input \(ax\) produces output \(ay\), where \(a\) is a constant.
    • Superposition: if input \(x_1\) produces output \(y_1\) and input \(x_2\) produces output \(y_2\), then input \((x_1 + x_2)\) produces output \((y_1 + y_2)\).

  • Charges: opposite charges attract each other; like charges (whether positive or negative) repel each other.

  • Right-hand rule for the magnetic field around a current-carrying wire: point the thumb of your right hand in the direction of the current; your fingers curl in the direction of the magnetic field.

    \[\vec{H} = \frac{I}{2\pi r}\,\hat{\phi}\]

  • Power in watts (W) in an electrical circuit is

    \[P = VI = I^2R = \frac{V^2}{R}\]

    where \(V\) is voltage in volts and \(I\) is current in amperes.

  • Instantaneous power: \(p(t) = v(t)\,i(t)\)

  • Time-average power over one period \(T\):

    \[P_{\text{avg}} = \frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T} p(t)\,dt\]

  • Kirchhoff’s Current Law: the sum of all currents entering a node equals the sum of all currents leaving that node.

  • Kirchhoff’s Voltage Law: the sum of all voltage drops and rises around any closed loop in a circuit must be equal to zero.

Complex Numbers and Phasors

  • Euler’s formula: \[e^{j\theta} = \cos\theta + j\sin\theta\]

  • Rectangular form: \(z = a + jb\)

  • Polar form: \(z = re^{j\theta}\) or \(z = r\angle\theta\)

  • Complex conjugate: \(z^* = a - jb\) or \(z^* = re^{-j\theta}\). Multiplying a complex number by its conjugate gives the square of its magnitude: \(zz^* = r^2\).

Phasors

  • For a signal \(v(t) = V_o\cos(\omega t + \phi)\), the phasor is \(\vec{V} = V_o e^{j\phi}\).
  • \(\sin(\theta) = \cos(\theta - \pi/2)\)

\[z = a + jb = r e^{j\theta},\quad \theta = \tan^{-1}(b/a)\]

Useful Numerical Values

  • \(\pi \approx 3.14,\quad 2\pi \approx 6.28,\quad \dfrac{1}{2\pi} \approx 0.159\)
  • \(\sqrt{2} \approx 1.414,\quad \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \approx 0.707\)
  • \(\log_{10}(2) \approx 0.301\)
  • \(e \approx 2.718,\quad \dfrac{1}{e} \approx 0.368,\quad 1 - \dfrac{1}{e} \approx 0.632,\quad 1 - e^{-5} \approx 0.993\)

Passive Filters

  • The complex impedance of a circuit element is

    \[Z = R + jX,\]

    where \(R\) is the resistance (real part) and \(X\) is the reactance (imaginary part).

  • Decibels provide a logarithmic measure of signal ratios:

    \[\text{dB} = 20\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{V_{\text{out}}}{V_{\text{in}}}\right),\qquad \text{dB} = 10\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{P_{\text{out}}}{P_{\text{in}}}\right).\]

  • First-order low-pass filter: \[H_{LP}(j\omega) = \frac{1}{1 + j\omega\tau}\]

  • First-order high-pass filter: \[H_{HP}(j\omega) = \frac{j\omega\tau}{1 + j\omega\tau}\]

  • In both cases, the time constant is \(\tau\), and the cutoff frequency is

    \[f_c = \frac{1}{2\pi\tau}.\]

Operational Amplifiers

Inverting operational amplifier

\[v_o = -\frac{R_2}{R_1}\,v_{in}\]

Non-inverting operational amplifier

\[v_o = \left(1 + \frac{R_2}{R_1}\right)v_{in}\]

Sampling

  • Nyquist sampling rule: to faithfully reconstruct a signal from discrete samples, the sampling frequency \(f_s\) must be at least twice the highest frequency \(f_{max}\) present in the signal:

    \[f_s \geq 2\,f_{max}\]

    The maximum frequency that can be represented at a given sampling rate is the Nyquist frequency \(f_N = f_s / 2\). Frequency content above \(f_N\) causes aliasing — it folds back into the sampled signal as a false lower frequency.
