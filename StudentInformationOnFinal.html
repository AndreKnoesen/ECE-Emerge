Example Questions

The examples below are taken from the 2025 final and illustrate the style and depth expected at each level. The 2026 final will draw from the same topic areas listed above.

Short Answer — Fundamental (Multiple Choice)

Multiple-choice questions evaluating basic recall of definitions, fundamental concepts, and simple relationships in electrical circuits.

What is the fundamental difference between energy and power in electrical systems?

a. Energy is the rate of power consumption.
b. Power is the capacity to do work, while energy is the rate at which it's done.
c. Energy is the total amount of work done, while power is the rate at which energy is transferred or converted.
d. Power is measured in joules, and energy is measured in watts.
e. There is no fundamental difference between energy and power.

Short Answer — Medium

Goes beyond simple recall. Explain how concepts relate to each other and apply them to a specific situation. Show a clear understanding of underlying mechanisms and key properties. Keep your response well-structured, typically 2 to 4 sentences.

What is the key property of a capacitor that differentiates its behavior from a resistor when a voltage is suddenly applied?

Answer: When voltage is first applied to a resistor, the current immediately becomes proportional to the applied voltage ($I = V/R$). A capacitor, however, resists changes in voltage. When voltage is first applied to a capacitor, it initially looks like a short circuit (allowing current to flow easily). The voltage across the capacitor starts at zero and increases gradually as it charges.

Short Answer — Advanced

Expects critical thinking and analysis. Define key principles relevant to the question, then show how different concepts connect. Use equations or diagrams where they help. Aim for 4 to 6 sentences combining theory with practical problem-solving.

What happens to the voltage across an inductor when the current flowing through it is suddenly interrupted?

Answer: When the current flowing through an inductor is suddenly interrupted, the inductor resists this sudden change in current by generating a voltage spike in the opposite direction. This voltage surge tries to maintain the current flow. In practical circuits, this "back EMF" can generate very high voltages, sometimes causing arcing across switch contacts or damaging components.

Essay — Medium Length

Focus on explaining fundamental circuit principles and how they apply in real situations. Connect different concepts clearly, structure your response, and show how circuit components interact and how mathematical principles support your reasoning.

Describe the concept of ground in circuit analysis, differentiate between a true ground connection to earth and a circuit ground reference, and explain why having a reference point is crucial for voltage measurements.

Answer: In circuit analysis, "ground" serves as a reference point for measuring voltages. A true ground connection to earth refers to a physical connection to the Earth's potential, often important for safety and for establishing a common reference in power distribution. A circuit ground reference, however, is a designated point within a circuit that is defined as having zero potential (0 V) for analysis purposes; it may or may not be physically connected to the Earth. Having a defined reference point is crucial because voltage is always measured as a difference in potential between two points. Without a specified reference, stating the voltage at a single point is ambiguous. By defining one point as ground (0 V), the voltage at any other point can be meaningfully measured as the potential difference between that point and ground.

Essay — Advanced Length

Apply circuit theory to complex or new situations. Connect different concepts, use strong problem-solving skills, and write in a structured way. Include equations or diagrams where they support your points.

Compare the energy storage mechanisms of inductors and capacitors, and explain how each component initially responds when a DC voltage is suddenly applied. Include the key equations that describe their behavior.

Answer: Inductors and capacitors are both passive components that store energy, but they do so through different mechanisms. Inductors store energy in a magnetic field when current flows through them, $E_L = \tfrac{1}{2} L i^2$. Capacitors store energy in an electric field when a voltage is applied across their plates, $E_C = \tfrac{1}{2} C v^2$. When a DC voltage is suddenly applied to an inductor (in series with a resistor), the inductor resists the sudden change in current; the current starts at zero and rises exponentially toward $I_{\text{final}} = V/R$, with the inductor voltage initially equal to the applied voltage and decaying to zero. The defining relationship is $v_L = L\,di/dt$. When a DC voltage is suddenly applied to a capacitor (in series with a resistor), the capacitor resists the sudden change in voltage; the voltage starts at zero and rises exponentially toward the applied voltage, while the current is initially maximum ($I_{\text{initial}} = V/R$) and decays to zero. The defining relationship is $i_C = C\,dv/dt$.
