A Note on the INA125 Output Load Resistor in Lab 8

ECE Emerge · Phase 1 Signal Conditioning · Spring 2026

The Lab 8 instructions contained a detail about Figure 1 that was not made explicit during the lab. The lab is done and nothing needs to be repeated. This note explains the detail and what it means as your team moves into the project.

What Figure 1 Shows — and Why

Figure 1 shows a 1 kΩ resistor connected from the INA125 output pin to ground. That resistor is a Stage 1 isolation test load. It is not a permanent part of the signal chain.

When the INA125 is first powered up in Stage 1, no downstream circuit is connected — no summing amplifier, no filter, nothing. The output pin has nowhere to send current. In a real breadboard circuit, an output with no load can behave unpredictably: floating rail currents and parasitic paths through the breadboard create an undefined load, and the output voltage may not settle correctly. Connecting a 1 kΩ resistor to ground gives the INA output a defined, stable current sink so the output voltage settles to a clean DC level and the gain can be measured reliably.

Think of the 1 kΩ as a stand-in that mimics the downstream circuit while the downstream circuit does not yet exist.

What Should Have Happened at the Stage 1 → Stage 2 Transition

Before wiring the summing amplifier in Stage 2, the 1 kΩ load resistor should have been removed. Once $R_a$ of the summing amplifier is connected to the INA output, $R_a$ serves as the INA's load. The 1 kΩ is no longer needed and should not be in the circuit.

The Lab 8 instructions did not flag this explicitly. This note is the correction.

The Problem: What Happens If the 1 kΩ Stays In

The figure below shows the INA125 output connected to the summing amplifier with the 1 kΩ test load still in place.

Figure: INA125 output simultaneously driving $R_a$ (to the summing amplifier virtual ground, held at $\approx 0\,\text{V}$ by op-amp negative feedback) and the 1 kΩ test load (to actual ground, exactly 0 V). Both resistors draw current from $v_\text{INA}$. Only the current through $R_a$ contributes to the summing amplifier output; the 1 kΩ current is wasted.

The essential point is that the summing amplifier's inverting input is a virtual ground: op-amp feedback forces it to sit at $\approx 0\,\text{V}$, the same potential as the actual ground rail. From the INA output's perspective, the far end of $R_a$ and the far end of the 1 kΩ both look like 0 V. Both resistors therefore draw current:

$$I_{R_a} = \frac{v_\text{INA}}{R_a}, \qquad I_{1\,\text{k}\Omega} = \frac{v_\text{INA}}{1\,\text{k}\Omega}.$$

Only $I_{R_a}$ flows through the summing amplifier and produces the output voltage. $I_{1\,\text{k}\Omega}$ flows uselessly to ground — it contributes nothing to $v_\text{sum}$ and draws extra current from the INA output stage.

The total load the INA must drive is the parallel combination $R_a \| 1\,\text{k}\Omega$. For the typical prelab range ($R_a = 10\,\text{k}\Omega$ to $100\,\text{k}\Omega$), this parallel combination is dominated by the smaller value:

$$R_a \,\|\, 1\,\text{k}\Omega \;=\; \frac{R_a \cdot 1\,\text{k}\Omega}{R_a + 1\,\text{k}\Omega} \;\approx\; 1\,\text{k}\Omega \quad \text{(since } R_a \gg 1\,\text{k}\Omega\text{)}.$$

The INA125's output swing specification — $\pm 4\,\text{V}$ on $\pm 5\,\text{V}$ supply rails — is guaranteed for output loads of 2 kΩ and above. Below 2 kΩ, the output stage cannot sustain the full swing: the INA begins to clip at a lower output voltage than the gain formula predicts, especially at the high end of its output range where the signal from a loaded bridge lives.

In the assembled chain (Stage 3 onward), a reduced INA output swing narrows the window available to the summing amplifier. The summing amp maps a smaller INA swing to a smaller span at the ADC: the $+2.5\,\text{V}$ and $-2.5\,\text{V}$ endpoints your prelab predicted will be shifted inward, and the full 5 V ADC window will not be used.

What This Means for the Project

On the solderless and soldered prototype, the 1 kΩ test load does not exist. There is no isolation-test resistor on the prototype board. The only load on the INA125 output is $R_a$ of the summing amplifier — a value well above the 2 kΩ minimum load specification. This means the INA's full $\pm 4\,\text{V}$ output swing is available from the start.

What to expect when you bring up the soldered chain. If your breadboard measurements showed the INA output or the summing amplifier output falling short of the prelab endpoint predictions, the 1 kΩ parallel load is a likely contributor. On the prototype you should see the chain track the design values more closely, all else being equal. If your breadboard and prototype results diverge in this direction — prototype swings wider, endpoints closer to $\pm 2.5\,\text{V}$ — that is expected and explainable.

Adjust your knobs on the prototype with this in mind. The four knobs ($R_g$, $R_f/R_a$, $R_f/R_b$, $R$ and $C$) that the lab taught you to name will behave as the design predicts on the prototype, because the INA is now driving its intended load. If you were tempted to compensate for a narrower-than-expected swing on the breadboard by changing $R_g$ or $R_f/R_a$, start from the prelab values on the prototype before making any adjustments.

The general principle to carry forward. Whenever you test a single stage of a multi-stage circuit in isolation, give the output a load that approximates what the next stage will present. When the stages are cascaded, the downstream circuit is the load. This applies throughout Phase 2 and beyond: if you ever need to test a sub-section of the soldered chain in isolation for debugging, provide a representative load resistor at the test point — and remember to remove it before reconnecting the downstream stage.
